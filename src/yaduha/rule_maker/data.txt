**1. Pattern Summary**

From the provided English–Paiute sentence pairs, several linguistic patterns can be observed:

- **Word Order**: Paiute generally follows a Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) order, whereas English typically uses Subject-Verb-Object (SVO).
- **Subject Pronouns**: The subject pronouns in Paiute appear to be "nü" for "I", "taa" for "you and I", "taagwa" for "we", and "uhuw̃a" for "they".
- **Object Markers**: The suffix "-noka" is used to mark the object in Paiute, as seen in "wai-noka" for "that rice" and "isha'pugu-noka" for "those dogs".
- **Verb Conjugation and Tense**: 
  - The suffix "-ti" indicates a present continuous action (e.g., "yotsi-ti" for "is flying").
  - The suffix "-gaa-wei" indicates a future action (e.g., "u-gwana-gaa-wei" for "is going to smell").
  - The suffix "-pü" indicates a completed action or past tense (e.g., "ui-düka-pü" for "have eaten").
  - The suffix "-wei" can also indicate a future action, as seen in "ui-dama'i-wei" for "will find".
  - The suffix "-ku" indicates a simple past action (e.g., "ui-dama'i-ku" for "found").
- **Possession and Plurality**: The use of "noka" seems to indicate a specific object, possibly with a sense of definiteness or plurality, as seen in "wai-noka" and "isha'pugu-noka".

**2. Translation Rules**

1. **Identify the Subject**: Determine the subject of the sentence and use the appropriate Paiute pronoun:
   - "I" -> "nü"
   - "You and I" -> "taa"
   - "We" -> "taagwa"
   - "They" -> "uhuw̃a"

2. **Identify the Object**: Translate the object and add the suffix "-noka" to indicate definiteness or specificity.

3. **Translate the Verb**: 
   - For present continuous actions, use the suffix "-ti".
   - For future actions, use the suffix "-gaa-wei" or "-wei".
   - For completed actions, use the suffix "-pü".
   - For simple past actions, use the suffix "-ku".

4. **Construct the Sentence**: Arrange the sentence in Subject-Object-Verb order.
