
## 1. Pattern Summary

Upon analyzing the provided English–Paiute sentence pairs, several linguistic patterns emerge:

- **Word Order**: Paiute sentences generally follow a Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) order, contrasting with the English Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order.
- **Subject and Object Markers**: The subject and object in Paiute are often marked with suffixes like "-noka" for objects and "-ii" or "-uu" for subjects.
- **Verb Conjugation and Tense**: Verbs in Paiute are modified with suffixes to indicate tense and aspect:
  - "-gaa-wei" indicates a future or intended action.
  - "-ti" indicates a present continuous action.
  - "-pü" indicates a completed action (past tense).
  - "-ku" is used for past actions as well.
  - "-wei" can also indicate a future action.
- **Pronouns and Plurality**: Pronouns such as "nü" (I), "taa" (you and I), "taagwa" (we), and "uhuw̃a" (they) are used to indicate the subject. Plurality is often implied in the context or through the use of pronouns.
- **Possession and Demonstratives**: Demonstratives like "wai-noka" (that rice) and "isha'pugu-noka" (those dogs) are used to specify objects.

## 2. Translation Rules

### Rule 1: Word Order
- Translate English SVO to Paiute SOV.
  - Example: "The jackrabbit is going to smell the mountain." → "kamü-uu toyabi-noka u-gwana-gaa-wei"

### Rule 2: Subject and Object Markers
- Use "-noka" to mark objects.
- Use "-ii" or "-uu" to mark subjects.
  - Example: "This cottontail hears that wood." → "tabuutsi'-ii küna-noka u-naka-dü"

### Rule 3: Verb Conjugation and Tense
- Use "-gaa-wei" for future tense or intended actions.
- Use "-ti" for present continuous actions.
- Use "-pü" or "-ku" for past actions.
- Use "-wei" for future actions.
  - Example: "I am going to eat that rice." → "wai-noka nüü ui-düka-gaa-wei"

### Rule 4: Pronouns and Plurality
- Use "nü" for "I", "taa" for "you and I", "taagwa" for "we", and "uhuw̃a" for "they".
  - Example: "You and I have eaten those rice." → "wai-noka taa ui-düka-pü"

### Rule 5: Possession and Demonstratives
- Use "wai-noka" for "that rice" and "isha'pugu-noka" for "those dogs".
  - Example: "We are going to find those dogs." → "isha'pugu-noka taagwa ui-dama'i-gaa-wei"
